All eight fields at once (multiple correspondence analysis). Categories that often appear in the same cases sit close together; grey dots are cases. The axes are computed from the data, not chosen: at each end you read the categories that define it most. Families group cases by similarity: two cases end up in the same family when they share many of their categories, and sharing a rare category counts more than sharing a common one. Nobody assigns a case to a family by hand; the number of families (five) is a reading choice, not an absolute result, and cases near the border between two families could fall in either. Hover a case to see its categories; click a category or a family to highlight its cases. Scroll to zoom, drag to move.

A rotating solid. 8-ray star: the eight fields are rays leaving the centre towards the corners of a cube; along each ray its categories go from the most common (near the centre) to the rarest (far out). Each case is a deformed cube touching its eight categories, so typical cases stay small at the centre and unusual cases stick out. About this project

Mapping time
through design

This database collects and organizes design artifacts — objects, interfaces, systems, and images — that engage with the representation, manipulation, or experience of time. Cases span over two millennia, from ancient Greece to contemporary smart wearables.

Each entry is catalogued across multiple analytical dimensions: the domain and type of the artifact, its technological condition, the sensory modality it addresses, and the specific temporal operations it performs.

Data Fields

  • Case Name Title of the artifact or system
  • Author / Producer Designer, firm, or institution
  • Date Year of creation or publication
  • Artifact Domain Object, image, 2D/3D digital interface…
  • Artifact Type Calendar, wristwatch, game, platform…
  • Temporal Focus Category of temporal operation
  • Temporal Operation Signaling, organizing, repeating…
  • Representation of Time How time is shown or encoded
  • Temporal Orientation Past, present, future or combinations
  • Design Intent Awareness, optimization, estimation…
  • Design Solution The design approach adopted
  • Sensory Modality Vision, touch, sound…
  • Technological Condition Analog or digital
  • Interaction Mode Static, dynamic, interactive

How to use

Case Studies gives a visual overview and supports multi-filter research. Data Viz offers six views, one at a time: Overview shows how cases are distributed in each field; Alluvial shows how the cases of one field flow into another; Matrix counts every combination of two fields and shows the empty ones; Timeline places cases in time, by category; Correspondence map places all eight fields on one plane and groups cases into families with a similar profile; 3D shows the eight fields as a rotating solid, or the correspondence map in three dimensions.

Click any tag in Cards to filter by that value. Case titles link to their original source.
